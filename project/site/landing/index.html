Four games, one word list

Every game works from the same words, so a word you miss in the quiz shows up again in the cards. Pick a language and try them below. The demo has 12 words. In the app you add your own.

Pick the right translation

You see a word and choose what it means. New words start with two options. As a word becomes familiar, more options appear, up to six, so a word you know well has to be picked out of more choices.

Quiz

Build the word piece by piece

The word is cut into pieces and you put them back in order. After a few right answers, decoy pieces from other words join in. It plays like a word scramble game, except every word has a meaning attached.

Wordcraft

Collect the letters

Steer the snake through the letters of the word in the right order. A wrong letter ends the run. You have to look at every letter, which helps with spelling.

Snake

Flip the card, then see it again later

Classic flashcards with a schedule behind them. A word you missed comes back in minutes. A word you knew waits longer each time: 5 minutes, 15 minutes, a few hours, a day, a week, up to eight months.

Flashcards

Want your own words? You can paste a list or type it in the app. The app is coming to this site soon.

Why the words stay

Words stick when you pull them out of memory yourself.

Each round asks you to recall a word: pick the meaning, rebuild the spelling, steer to the right letter. The schedule decides when you need the word next. Missed words return soon and known words return much later, so your time goes to the words that need it.

Questions

Are vocabulary games a good way to learn words?

They work best when the game makes you recall the word yourself. Here you choose, build or spell the word, so every round is a small recall. Games don't replace reading and speaking, but they give you many repetitions without much boredom.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no account to create and there are no ads.

Which languages can I learn?

The demo on this page has eight. In the app you can add words in any language and write the translation in yours.

Can I use my own words?

Yes, in the app. Paste a list, type words one by one, or paste columns from a spreadsheet.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. It runs in a mobile browser and you can add it to your home screen. Laptops and desktops work too.

Does it work offline?

After the first load, yes. Your progress stays on the device and you can move it to another one with a sync code.

Who is it for?

Anyone learning a language, and anyone who has a word list to learn, for example for the SAT, TOEFL or GRE. The app doesn't ship ready-made exam lists, so you add your own.
